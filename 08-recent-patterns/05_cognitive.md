# 05. Cognitive Games & Behavioral Reported Patterns

This document covers the gamified aptitude challenges (Switch, Motion, Digit, Grid) and forced-choice behavioral dilemmas.

## Reported Pattern Archetypes

### 1. Switch Challenge: Composite Permutations & Anchor Fast-Scan
- **Reported In**: Video Debriefs
- **Teached By**: `COG-024` to `COG-038`
- **Pattern Details**: The puzzle provides an input sequence of 4 geometric symbols/digits, passes them through two unknown permutation operators, and shows the output.
- **Solving Strategy**: Track an anchor element (e.g. unique shape or digit '4'). If '4' moves from position 1 to position 3 in the first layer, eliminate all operator choices that do not map position 1 to 3.

### 2. Motion Challenge: Friction-Free Maze Navigation
- **Reported In**: Video Debriefs
- **Teached By**: `COG-001` to `COG-007`
- **Pattern Details**: A ball slides across an ice grid without stopping until it collides with a wall or obstacle block.
- **Solving Strategy**: Work backwards from target hole. Identify the stopping block directly aligned with the goal, then maneuver secondary movable blocks into position to serve as backstops.

### 3. Deductive Reasoning: Syllogistic Traps
- **Reported In**: Video Debriefs
- **Teached By**: `COG-015` to `COG-023`, `COG-051` to `COG-071`
- **Pattern Details**:
  - *Conversion Trap*: "All A are B" does NOT mean "All B are A".
  - *Particular Trap*: "Some A are B" does NOT mean "Some A are not B".
  - *Negative Trap*: Two negative premises yield no definite conclusion.
  - *Complementary Pair*: 'Some A are B' and 'No A is B' between unlinked terms form an 'Either/Or' pair.

### 4. Behavioral Forced-Choice: Consistency Profiling
- **Reported In**: Video Debriefs
- **Teached By**: `BEH-001` to `BEH-025`
- **Pattern Details**: Pairs of equally desirable statements (Statement A vs Statement B) test organizational values (Innovation vs SLA compliance; Autonomy vs Team consensus).
- **Core Rule**: Delivery reliability, customer empathy, coachability, and peer collaboration are generally safer heuristics, but maintaining strict consistency across disguised versions is the primary scoring metric.
